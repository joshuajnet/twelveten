Twelve Ten Gallery is pleased to present Micah Schippa-Wildfong and Noelle Africh in the inaugural edition of Neighbors Chicago, featuring works that thoughtfully engage with the distinct domestic architecture of the space. Each artist's work uniquely references materials commonly found and historically situated in the home, but through their practices they highlight a sense of loss and the absence of presence.

Micah Schippa-Wildfong, "american sleep", 2024-ongoing. Lights collected from various locations of purpose, each set to an automatic incremental timer. Dimensions variable. Photo credit: Mikey Mosher.

Micah Schippa-Wildfong's "american sleep" (2024-ongoing) expands on the artist's past performance for the Gray Center for Arts and Inquiry and its recent exhibition at Twelve Ten Gallery. Consisting of a constellation of lights set to various timers, the installation presents us with a synecdoche of the electrical grid, a large, mostly invisible and abstract structure that people interact with in their rhythms of daily life but rarely consider. As in much of the artist's work, found objects are submitted to a set of procedures determined by the artist, but then left to develop from those initial conditions, taking on an active and poetic quality. Here the lights continue their performance, winking on and off in staggered rhythms, absent the ghostly subjects they allude to.

Noelle Africh's "Late Season" (2025) is a small distemper painting that reimagines a historically decorative medium through an experimental, abstract lens. Rather than adhering to traditional painterly approaches, Africh treats distemper as a process akin to photographic chemical development, allowing the image to emerge through reactive transformation. When first applied, distemper initially produces a surface that appears cloudy and unsettled, resisting fixed form. Africh heightens this instability by intervening as the material begins to cure, reactivating and disrupting the surface. Each gesture both alters and obscures what came before, setting in motion a chain of partial revelations. From these contingent processes, the image gradually materializes, less as a fixed composition than as a spectral presence, hovering at the threshold of visibility.
